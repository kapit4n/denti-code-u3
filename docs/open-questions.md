| 17  | May an appointment be booked with a dentist or chair that is **not active**? | **Answered: no.** `createAppointment` and `rescheduleAppointment` refuse an inactive clinician or chair with `UNBOOKABLE_RESOURCE` (409 `DOMAIN_RULE_VIOLATION`), and the message names the resource. `onlyActive` still defaults to _no filter_ on the two list endpoints — a clinician who has left stays visible on the appointments they worked.                                                                                                                     | Reschedule of an appointment whose clinician was deactivated _afterwards_ is refused too, so the booking has to be reassigned. | 18 | What happens when a clinician re-opens a closed visit? | **Partly answered.** `reopenVisit` puts the visit back to `OPEN` and clears `ended_at` (ADR 0022). Until then a re-opening is silent, which is the honest cost of building it now rather than the whole trail. | Milestone 12 |

| 19 | May the **same patient hold two open visits at once**? A patient cannot literally be in two chairs, and the rule "refuse a second open visit for the same patient" may well be right. | **Assumed: no rule yet — two open visits are currently allowed.** It applies to _both_ doors (`startVisit` and the walk-in), and the appointment door shipped without it, so adding it to the walk-in alone (ADR 0024) would make the two doors disagree. `findOpenForPatient` already exists in the repository, written and waiting for this rule or its caller. | The first clinic to ask for it gets both doors changed at once, in one slice, with the existing repository method doing the reading. Implementing it now on one door would ship a configurable disagreement instead. | The visit workspace (Milestone 6), where an open visit is visible and the question becomes real. |

| 20 | Once a visit's charges have been **settled into an invoice**, may the front desk record a **follow-up payment** against the same visit — a patient who pays by instalments? | **Answered: yes (Milestone 9, slice 3 — invoice-scoped follow-up payments).** When all charges on a visit are already on documents and at least one of those documents still has a balance, `payVisitCharges` collects the remainder across the visit's open documents oldest first, allocating money and advancing each document's status as its balance reaches zero. The rule is enforced inside the same transaction; the workspace's Payments section opens the door in that case and `createPayment` still refetches both registers. The refusal "There is nothing left to pay on this visit" remains only when nothing here is owed. | Follow-up payments land on the visit's own documents (not loose to the patient's ledger via some other path), oldest first, with `updateStatus` moving `ISSUED`→`PARTIALLY_PAID`→`PAID`. A void/draft document and a fully settled document are not collectible. | Nothing outstanding for this question. |

| 21 | What should front-desk roles (`RECEPTIONIST`, `ASSISTANT`) see in the **visit directory**? A clinician sees their own; the manager sees the clinic's. | **Assumed: the whole clinic**, the same rows `visits:read` already lets them open by id. Narrowing the list would invent a barrier the detail route does not enforce. | If the clinic wants the front desk limited to visits they started, the schema needs a "started by" column on `visits` (today `created_by` is untyped text) and the domain scope rule grows a case. See [ADR 0031](./decisions/0031-the-visit-directory-is-scoped-to-the-caller.md). | When a front-desk user says the directory is too broad — or if the audit-log question (15) gives `created_by` a real meaning. |

**Rule:** do not build any of these speculatively. Resolve them at the milestone
where they become blocking, and write an ADR if the answer changes an
architectural decision.
